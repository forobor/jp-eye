## AのB — possessive の

**Seen: 4**

The particle **の** (no) joins two nouns. The noun after の is the main thing, and the noun
before it describes or owns it.

> **A の B** = "B of A" / "A's B"

English often puts it the other way around ("guardian angel **of desire**"), but Japanese
always puts the describing part first.

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 欲望の守護天使 | yokubō no shugo tenshi | desire の guardian angel | guardian angel of desire |
| 実在の人物 | jitsuzai no jinbutsu | real-existence の person | real people |
| 私の剣 | watashi no ken | I の sword | my sword |

## い-adjective + noun

**Seen: 1**

An **い-adjective** (one ending in い, like 黒い "black") goes **directly before the noun**,
in its normal dictionary form. You don't need の or anything else in between.

> **[い-adjective] + [noun]**

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 黒い剣士 | kuroi kenshi | black swordsman | the black swordsman |
| 大きい剣 | ōkii ken | big sword | a big sword |

Note: the い at the end is written in hiragana. This part (the *okurigana*) changes when the
adjective is conjugated, for example 黒**くない** "not black" or 黒**かった** "was black".

## AはBです — "A is B"

**Seen: 2**

The most basic Japanese sentence.

> **A は B です** = "A is B" (literally "as for A, it is B")

- **は** marks the **topic**, the thing the sentence is about. It's written は (ha) but
  read **wa** when it's used as a particle.
- **です** is the polite "is / am / are". Its plain (casual) form is **だ**.
- The verb always comes **at the end** of a Japanese sentence.

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| この作品はフィクションです | kono sakuhin wa fikushon desu | this work [topic] fiction is | This work is fiction. |
| 彼は剣士です | kare wa kenshi desu | he [topic] swordsman is | He is a swordsman. |
| かかったのはてめえだ | kakatta no wa temee da | fell-for-it one [topic] you is | The one who fell for it is you. (casual だ) |

## この / その / あの + noun

**Seen: 7**

These point at things, and a noun always comes after them.

| Word | Meaning | Where it is |
|---|---|---|
| **こ**の | this | near me (the speaker) |
| **そ**の | that | near you (the listener) |
| **あ**の | that over there | far from both of us |
| **ど**の | which? | question |

The こ / そ / あ / ど pattern repeats in other words too: これ / それ / あれ / どれ
("this one, that one…") and ここ / そこ / あそこ / どこ ("here, there…").

| Japanese | Romaji | Natural |
|---|---|---|
| この作品 | kono sakuhin | this work |
| あの剣士 | ano kenshi | that swordsman (over there) |

## A・B・Cなど — listing with など

**Seen: 1**

- **・** (nakaguro, a middle dot) separates items in a list, like a comma.
- **など** after the list means "and so on, etc.", so the list isn't complete.

| Japanese | Romaji | Natural |
|---|---|---|
| 人物・団体・事件など | jinbutsu, dantai, jiken nado | people, organizations, events, etc. |

## には — に + は

**Seen: 2**

**に** marks a target or place ("to, at, in"). Adding **は** makes that target the topic:
"as for (to) X…". It's very common before a negative, where it means "with X, it's
**not** the case".

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 事件などには関係ありません | jiken nado ni wa kankei arimasen | events etc. to [topic] relation there-is-not | It has nothing to do with real events etc. |

## ～ません — polite negative

**Seen: 1**

Polite verbs end in **～ます**. To make them negative, change it to **～ません**.

| Dictionary | Polite | Polite negative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ある | あります | **ありません** | there is / there is not |
| 食べる | 食べます | **食べません** | eat / don't eat |
| 行く | 行きます | **行きません** | go / don't go |

Note: **関係ありません** literally means "relation does not exist", so "has nothing to do
with…". It's a very common phrase.

**Seen: 1**

**いっさい** (一切, usually written in hiragana) with a negative verb means "not at all,
absolutely none". It makes the negative much stronger.

| Japanese | Romaji | Natural |
|---|---|---|
| いっさい関係ありません | issai kankei arimasen | no connection whatsoever |

## ～た — plain (casual) past

**Seen: 2**

The casual way to say something happened. Polite speech uses ～ました, while friends, enemies
and inner thoughts use ～た.

How it's made depends on how the verb ends. Here's the first group:

| Verb ends in | Past ends in | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| る (u-verb) | **った** | かか**る** → かか**った** | got caught |
| う | **った** | 買**う** → 買**った** | bought |
| つ | **った** | 待**つ** → 待**った** | waited |

(More endings will be added as they show up in the game.)

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| かかったな | kakatta na | got-caught な | You fell for it! |
| かかったのは | kakatta no wa | got-caught one [topic] | the one who fell for it |

## Sentence-ending な

**Seen: 1**

**な** at the very end of a sentence adds rough emphasis, a bit like "huh!", "so…!" or
"I knew it!". It sounds tough or masculine and is typical of villains, warriors and old men.

| Japanese | Romaji | Natural |
|---|---|---|
| かかったな | kakatta na | You fell for it, huh! |

Don't confuse it with the **な** that comes after the dictionary form of a verb, which is a
rough "don't!" (e.g. 行くな "don't go!").

## Dropped subject

**Seen: 8**

Japanese usually **leaves out "I / you / he / she"** when context makes it clear. The
listener fills it in.

| Japanese | Literal | What's understood |
|---|---|---|
| かかったな | fell-for-it な | **You** fell for it! |

So when translating, ask yourself "who is doing this?" The answer is usually the speaker or
the person being spoken to.

## Stretched sounds in manga (ォォォ, ー, っ)

**Seen: 5**

Not really grammar, but it's everywhere in manga and games:

- **Extra vowels**, often small katakana (ォ, ァ, ッ) or **ー**: the sound is stretched,
  like a shout or scream. 小僧**ォォォ** = "brat-**aaargh**!"
- **Small っ at the end** (e.g. くそっ): the sound is cut off sharply, as in anger or surprise.
- **!!** and **!?** work like in English, only stronger.

Just ignore the extra vowels to find the real word: 小僧ォォォ is **小僧**.

## を — object marker

**Seen: 1**

**を** marks the **object**, the thing the verb is done to. It's written を (wo) but read
**o**.

> **[thing] を [verb]**

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 天国をあじわう | tengoku o ajiwau | heaven を taste | taste heaven |
| 剣を使う | ken o tsukau | sword を use | use a sword |

## Verb stem + ながら — "while doing"

**Seen: 1**

Take the **ます-stem** of a verb (the polite form without ます) and add **ながら** to get
"while doing X". The same person does both actions, and the **last verb is the main action**.

| Dictionary | Polite (ます) | Stem + ながら | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| あじわう | あじわいます | **あじわいながら** | while tasting |
| 食べる | 食べます | **食べながら** | while eating |
| 見る | 見ます | **見ながら** | while watching |

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 天国をあじわいながら地獄へ落ちる | tengoku o ajiwai nagara jigoku e ochiru | heaven を tasting-while hell へ fall | fall into hell while tasting heaven |

---

## へ — direction "to, toward"

**Seen: 1**

**へ** after a place means "to, toward". It's written へ (he) but read **e** when it's used
as a particle.

| Japanese | Romaji | Natural |
|---|---|---|
| 地獄へ落ちる | jigoku e ochiru | fall to hell |
| 国へ帰る | kuni e kaeru | go back to (one's) country |

に can usually be used in the same place (地獄に落ちる). へ puts a little more feeling on the
direction or movement.

## Verb (dictionary form) + がいい — "go ahead and…!"

**Seen: 1**

**[verb, dictionary form] + がいい** literally means "[doing it] is good". In practice it's an
**arrogant command or curse**: "go ahead and…", "you may as well…". It's typical of villains,
demons and kings.

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 地獄へ落ちるがいい | jigoku e ochiru ga ii | hell へ fall が good | Go ahead and fall into hell! |
| 死ぬがいい | shinu ga ii | die が good | Just die! |

Compare it with the polite advice form **～たほうがいい** ("you'd better…"). That one will be
added when it shows up.

## Verb + の(は) — "the one who / the thing that"

**Seen: 1**

Put **の** after a plain verb and the verb becomes a **noun**: "the one who…" or "the thing
that…".

> **[verb] の は [Y] だ** = "The one who [verb] is **Y**"

This pattern puts the focus on **Y**, the answer, which comes last.

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| かかったのはてめえだ | kakatta no wa temee da | fell-for-it の は you だ | **You're** the one who fell for it! |
| 落ちるのはお前だ | ochiru no wa omae da | fall の は you だ | **You're** the one who's going to fall! |

Note: this is a different job from the possessive **AのB** (欲望の守護天使). Look at what comes
before の. A noun means "of", and a verb means "the one who".

## Sentence-ending よ

**Seen: 2**

**よ** at the end of a sentence means "I'm telling you!" or "you know!". The speaker uses it to
give the listener **new information** or to **insist** on something.

| Japanese | Romaji | Natural |
|---|---|---|
| てめえだよ | temee da yo | It's YOU, I tell you! |
| いいよ | ii yo | It's fine, really! |

Compare it with **な** (already in the list). な is more like "huh!" to yourself, while よ is
pushed at the listener.

## Rough speech: あい / あえ → ええ

**Seen: 4**

Tough, rude or angry speakers (thugs, warriors, rough heroes) merge some vowel pairs into a long
**ええ**:

| Normal | Rough | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| てまえ (手前) | **てめえ** | you (very rude) |
| ない | **ねえ** | not |
| うるさい | **うるせえ** | shut up / noisy |
| すごい | **すげえ** | amazing |

When you see an odd word ending in **ええ**, try changing it back to **あい / あえ** to find the
dictionary word.

## Verb + んじゃねえ — rough "Don't…!"

**Seen: 1**

Put **んじゃない** after a verb in dictionary form to angrily tell someone **not** to do it. Rough speakers say **んじゃねえ**.

> Verb (dictionary form) + んじゃない / んじゃねえ = "Don't ~!"

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| チョロチョロ動くんじゃねえ!! | Chorochoro ugokun ja nē!! | scurry move [it's that] is-not | Quit scurrying around!! |
| 泣くんじゃない。 | Nakun ja nai. | cry [it's that] is-not | Don't cry. |
| 逃げるんじゃねえ! | Nigerun ja nē! | run-away [it's that] is-not | Don't run away! |

## ～ない — plain negative

**Seen: 4**

The casual way to say a verb is **not** happening. For godan verbs, change the last **u**-sound to an **a**-sound, then add **ない**. For る-verbs, drop る and add ない.

> 当たる → 当たら + ない, 食べる → 食べ + ない

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 当たらねえ | ataranē | hit-not | (it) won't hit |
| 行かない | ikanai | go-not | (I) won't go |
| 食べない | tabenai | eat-not | (I) don't eat |

---

## ～だろうが — "…obviously, dammit!"

**Seen: 1**

**だろう** normally means "probably / right?". With **が** added at the end, it becomes an angry "isn't it obvious?!". The speaker is blaming the listener. It's rough speech.

> Sentence + だろうが! = "…obviously, dammit!"

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 当たらねえだろおがア!! | Ataranē darō gaa!! | hit-not probably [but] | I can't hit you, dammit!! |
| 危ないだろうが! | Abunai darō ga! | dangerous probably [but] | That's dangerous, you idiot! |

## Mimetic words (擬態語) — sound-picture words

**Seen: 1**

Japanese has many **mimetic words**. They describe how something moves or feels, not a real sound. They are often a doubled pair and are usually written in katakana in manga. They act like adverbs in front of verbs.

> Mimetic word + verb

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| チョロチョロ動く | chorochoro ugoku | scurry-scurry move | dart around |
| キラキラ光る | kirakira hikaru | sparkle-sparkle shine | sparkle |
| ドキドキする | dokidoki suru | thump-thump do | (heart) pounds |

## かわいそうに — "Poor thing!"

**Seen: 4**

かわいそう means "pitiful" or "poor". When you add **に** and say it on its own, it becomes a cry of pity, like English "Poor thing!" or "How sad!" It's often followed by the person or thing you feel sorry for.

> かわいそうに (+ who/what)

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| かわいそうに あのエルフ | kawaisō ni ano erufu | pitiful[ly] that elf | That poor elf... |
| かわいそうに。 | kawaisō ni. | pitiful[ly] | Poor thing. |
| かわいそうに、あの子 | kawaisō ni, ano ko | pitiful[ly], that child | That poor kid. |

---

## Inverted word order (倒置) — afterthought subject

**Seen: 2**

In normal Japanese the topic comes first: あのエルフはかわいそうだ. In speech, people often say the feeling first and add **who or what** it's about at the end, like a second thought. Particles such as は are usually dropped when they do this.

> Comment + Topic (no は)

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| かわいそうに あのエルフ | kawaisō ni ano erufu | pitiful[ly] that elf | That poor elf... |
| おいしいね、これ | oishii ne, kore | tasty [ne], this | This is tasty, huh. |
| すごいな、あいつ | sugoi na, aitsu | amazing [na], that guy | That guy's amazing. |

## かわいそうに — "Poor thing…" (exclamation of pity)

**Seen: 1**

かわいそう means "pitiful". With に added, it becomes a short exclamation: "Poor thing!" or "How sad!" The person or thing you feel sorry for often comes **after** it. It can be honest pity or sarcastic, mocking pity.

> かわいそうに(、 + person/thing)

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| かわいそうに あのエルフ | kawaisō ni ano erufu | pitiful [ly] that elf | Poor thing, that elf. |
| かわいそうに | kawaisō ni | pitiful [ly] | Poor thing! |
| かわいそうに、あの子 | kawaisō ni, ano ko | pitiful [ly], that kid | That poor kid. |

## Inverted word order (倒置) — feeling first, topic after

**Seen: 1**

In normal Japanese order, the topic comes first: あのエルフはかわいそうだ. In casual speech and manga, people often say the feeling first and add the topic after it. They also drop は and だ.

> comment + topic

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| かわいそうに あのエルフ | kawaisō ni ano erufu | pitiful [ni] that elf | Poor thing, that elf. |
| すごいな、これ。 | sugoi na, kore. | amazing [na], this | This is amazing. |
| うまいよ、このラーメン。 | umai yo, kono rāmen. | tasty [yo], this ramen | This ramen is good. |

## でも — "even" (after a noun)

**Seen: 1**

Put でも after a noun to say **"even"**, meaning that even this person or thing is included, which is surprising.

> Noun + でも

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 領主でも手が出せねえ | ryōshu demo te ga dasenē | lord even hand [subj] can't put out | Even the lord can't touch them |
| 子供でもわかる | kodomo demo wakaru | child even understands | Even a child gets it |
| 先生でも知らない | sensei demo shiranai | teacher even doesn't know | Even the teacher doesn't know |

## Potential form — ～える / ～られる (can do)

**Seen: 1**

The **potential form** means "can do". For godan verbs, change the last **-u** sound to **-eru**. For ichidan verbs, change る to られる. The object often takes **が** instead of を.

> 出す → 出せる (can put out)  ·  食べる → 食べられる (can eat)

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 手が出せねえ | te ga dasenē | hand [subj] can't put out | (We) can't lay a hand on them |
| 日本語が話せる | nihongo ga hanaseru | Japanese [subj] can speak | I can speak Japanese |
| 泳げない | oyogenai | can't swim | I can't swim |

## ～んだ — explaining "you see"

**Seen: 1**

Add **んだ** (casual form of のだ) after a plain verb or adjective. It shows you are **explaining** something or giving the reason behind a situation.

> Plain form + んだ

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 手が出せねえんだ | te ga dasenē n da | hand [subj] can't put out [explain] | They just can't be touched, you see |
| 疲れたんだ | tsukareta n da | got tired [explain] | I'm tired, that's why |
| 行くんだ | iku n da | go [explain] | I'm going, you see |

## ～ないほうがいい — "you'd better not"

**Seen: 1**

To give advice to **not** do something, use the ない-form + **ほうがいい** ("that side is good"). For positive advice, use the た-form + ほうがいい.

> Verb (ない-form) + ほうがいい

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 関わらない方がいい | kakawaranai hō ga ii | not get involved side [subj] good | You'd better not get involved |
| 行かないほうがいい | ikanai hō ga ii | not go side [subj] good | You'd better not go |
| 寝たほうがいい | neta hō ga ii | slept side [subj] good | You should get some sleep |

## どうしようもない — "nothing can be done"

**Seen: 1**

どうしようもない is a set phrase. It means **"it can't be helped"** or **"there's nothing to be done."** It is built from どう (how) + しよう ("shall I do", the volitional form of する) + も (even) + ない (not). So it literally says "there isn't even a 'how shall I do it'."

> どうしようもない (rough: どうしようもねえ)

You can also put it in front of a noun: どうしようもない人 means "a hopeless person".

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| どうしようもねえよ | dō shiyō mo nē yo | how do-(let's) [even] not [!] | There's nothing anyone can do about it. |
| 雨だから、どうしようもない。 | ame da kara, dō shiyō mo nai. | rain is because, how do-(let's) [even] not | It's raining, so it can't be helped. |
| どうしようもない人だ。 | dō shiyō mo nai hito da. | hopeless person is | He's a hopeless guy. |

## Sentence-ending ぞ

**Seen: 1**

ぞ goes at the very end of a sentence. It makes what you say sound strong and sure. Often you are announcing something, warning someone, or getting yourself fired up. It's mostly used by men and in rough, casual speech. It's stronger than よ.

> plain-form sentence + ぞ

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 次いくぞ | tsugi iku zo | next go [ぞ] | On to the next one! |
| 行くぞ！ | iku zo! | go [ぞ] | Let's go! |
| 危ないぞ | abunai zo | dangerous [ぞ] | Hey, that's dangerous! |

## Sentence-ending ぜ

**Seen: 1**

**ぜ** is like ぞ but a little more cocky and friendly-rough. It is mostly used by men in casual speech. It means something like "I'm telling you!" or "you know!"

## 上等だ — "Bring it on!" (accepting a challenge)

**Seen: 1**

上等 literally means "top quality". In rough speech, when someone threatens or challenges you, shouting **上等だ!** (or 上等じゃねえか!) means "That suits me fine, bring it on!"

> (threat) → 上等だ！ / 上等じゃねえか！

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 上等だあ このタコ!! | jōtō daa kono tako!! | first-class [is], this octopus!! | Bring it on, you idiot!! |
| 上等じゃねえか！ | jōtō ja nee ka! | first-class [isn't it]! | Fine by me, let's go! |
| やるのか？上等だ！ | yaru no ka? jōtō da! | do [?] first-class [is]! | You wanna fight? Bring it! |

## それじゃ(あ) — "well then"

**Seen: 1**

それじゃ (or それじゃあ) is the casual form of それでは. It means "well then" or "so." Use it to move on to the next thing or to start a request. Even shorter: じゃ / じゃあ.

> それじゃ(あ) + sentence

## あんた / ～ら — rough "you (guys)"

**Seen: 1**

あんた is a casual short form of あなた. It can sound rough or rude. Adding ら makes it plural, like "you guys." ～ら also works with other words for people: 俺ら (us), こいつら (these guys).

> あんた + ら = you guys

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| あんたらのボス | anta-ra no bosu | you-guys 's boss | your boss |
| あんた、誰？ | anta, dare? | you, who | Who are you? |
| 俺らは強い。 | ore-ra wa tsuyoi. | we as-for strong | We're strong. |

## 頼まれてくれる — "do me a favor"

**Seen: 1**

頼む means "to ask (a favor)." 頼まれる is the passive: "to be asked." But 頼まれてくれる is a fixed phrase: "please accept being asked by me" = "do me a favor." It sounds friendly and a bit old-style.

> (thing) + 頼まれてくれる？ = will you do (thing) for me?

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 言伝頼まれてくれないかな? | kotozute tanomarete kurenai ka na? | message be-asked give-not I-wonder | Could you take a message for me? |
| ちょっと頼まれてくれる？ | chotto tanomarete kureru? | a-bit be-asked give? | Can you do me a small favor? |

## ～てくれないかな — "could you … for me?"

**Seen: 1**

くれる means someone does something for me. くれない is its negative. Adding かな ("I wonder") makes a soft, casual request, like "I wonder if you could…"

> Verb て-form + くれないかな？

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 頼まれてくれないかな? | tanomarete kurenai ka na? | be-asked give-not I-wonder | Could you do this for me? |
| 手伝ってくれないかな？ | tetsudatte kurenai ka na? | help give-not I-wonder | Could you help me? |
| 待ってくれないかな？ | matte kurenai ka na? | wait give-not I-wonder | Could you wait for me? |

## Sentence-ending かな — "I wonder"

**Seen: 1**

かな goes at the end of a sentence. It means "I wonder…" It sounds casual and gentle. People often use it when talking to themselves or making soft requests.

> plain sentence + かな

| Japanese | Romaji | Literal | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 頼まれてくれないかな? | tanomarete kurenai ka na? | be-asked give-not I-wonder | Could you do me a favor? |
| 雨が降るかな。 | ame ga furu ka na. | rain falls I-wonder | I wonder if it'll rain. |
| これでいいかな？ | kore de ii ka na? | this with good I-wonder | Is this okay, I wonder? |
